Orbital Mechanics 101

Understanding Keplerian elements and how satellites are tracked

The Six Classical Orbital Elements (Keplerian)

ElementSymbolDescription
Semi-major axisaDefines orbit size. Period T = 2π√(a³/μ), where μ = 398,600 km³/s²
EccentricityeShape of orbit: 0 = circular, < 1 = elliptical. e = (apogee-perigee)/(apogee+perigee+2×R⊕)
InclinationiTilt of orbit plane relative to equator. 0° = equatorial, 98° = sun-synchronous (SSO)
RAANΩRight Ascension of Ascending Node — orientation of orbit in space
Argument of PerigeeωPosition of closest approach point within the orbit plane
True AnomalyνCurrent position along the orbit, measured from perigee

Orbital Regimes

LEO (160-2000 km): Home to ISS, Starlink, weather satellites. Orbital period ~90 min. Highest collision risk due to density.
MEO (2000-35786 km): GPS, GLONASS, Galileo navigation. Period ~6-12 hours.
GEO (35786 km): Communications, weather. Period = sidereal day (23h 56m). Must be disposed to graveyard orbit.
HEO (>36000 km): Highly elliptical orbits (Molniya, Tundra). Used for high-latitude coverage.

SGP4 Propagation

The Simplified General Perturbations 4 (SGP4) algorithm is the standard for propagating Two-Line Elements (TLEs). It models Earth's oblateness (J2 effect), atmospheric drag (via B* ballistic coefficient), and secular/periodic perturbations. For deep-space objects (period > 225 min), the SDP4 variant adds lunar-solar gravity and resonance effects.

Regulatory Standards & Compliance

International frameworks governing orbital debris mitigation

FCC 25-Year Rule (47 CFR §25.114)

The U.S. Federal Communications Commission requires satellite operators to submit debris mitigation plans. Non-GEO spacecraft must be disposed of within 25 years after mission completion. A proposed 5-year rule is under consideration for new licenses.

ISO 24113:2023 — Space Debris Mitigation

International standard defining requirements for limiting debris generation. Key provisions: probability of casualty from re-entry < 1:10,000; GEO disposal to graveyard orbit (300 km above GEO); passivation of energy sources (fuel vents, battery discharge).

IADC Space Debris Mitigation Guidelines

The Inter-Agency Space Debris Coordination Committee (IADC) provides consensus guidelines adopted by 13 space agencies. Forms the basis for national regulations including ESA's Space Debris Mitigation Policy.

About and Methodology

Platform Overview

The Orbital Intelligence Platform tracks 70,586 objects from Celestrak SATCAT, computes orbital positions using SGP4/SDP4 propagation, predicts atmospheric re-entry risk using an Ensemble Conjunction Classifier, and forecasts collision probability based on orbital density analysis.

Propagation Model

Algorithm: SGP4/SDP4 (Simplified General Perturbations)

Perturbation Model: JGM-3 Gravity Field

Data Source: Celestrak SATCAT (Two-Line Element format)

Re-entry Prediction - Ensemble Conjunction Classifier

Algorithm: Random Forest Classifier (100 decision trees, ensemble method)

Feature Set: Apogee (km), Perigee (km), Inclination (deg), Eccentricity, Orbital Period (min), Object Type, Owner/Country, Radar Cross-Section, Orbit Class, Launch Era

Target: IsDecayed (binary - atmospheric re-entry status)

Collision Risk Model

Collision risk computed via orbital density analysis: objects grouped into 50 km altitude bands. Local density (plus/minus 50 km) combined with orbit class multipliers (LEO=1.5x, GEO=1.2x) and type multipliers (Debris=1.3x, Rocket=1.2x). TCA estimated from orbital period; miss distance from conjunction geometry.

Predictive Scenario Model

Kessler Syndrome Projection: Projects debris growth from 2025-2035 under two scenarios: (1) Linear growth at 3%/year reflecting current launch rates and fragmentation events; (2) Accelerating cascade where each year post-2028 multiplies growth by 1.08x as collision fragments generate additional collisions. Model calibrated against historical fragmentation data (2007 Fengyun-1C, 2009 Iridium-Cosmos collision).

International Compliance: FCC 25-Year/5-Year post-mission disposal rules and ISO 24113 debris mitigation standards applied to assess object compliance status. LEO objects with high decay probability flagged as non-compliant; GEO objects assessed against graveyard orbit requirements.

Risk Classification Thresholds

  • Low - Score under 0.15: sparse orbit, collision unlikely
  • Medium - Score 0.15-0.30: moderate density, monitoring suggested
  • High - Score 0.30-0.60: crowded band, active monitoring required
  • Critical - Score over 0.60: dense conjunction zone, immediate attention

Limitations

  • Positions computed from SATCAT orbital parameters, not real-time TLE propagation
  • TCA and miss-distance are estimated from density modeling, not precise ephemeris
  • Model predicts statistical re-entry probability, not specific re-entry dates
  • Data from Celestrak SATCAT may not include classified objects

Glossary

Payload
A functional satellite deployed for communications, navigation, Earth observation, or research.
Rocket Body
Upper stage of a launch vehicle left in orbit after deploying payload. Uncontrolled - high collision risk.
Debris
Fragments from breakups, collisions, or degradation.
Conjunction Zone
Altitude band with high object density where collision probability is greatest.
Kessler Syndrome
Cascade where debris collisions create more debris, eventually making orbits unusable.
TCA
Time-to-Closest Approach - estimated time until two objects reach minimum separation.
ColA
Collision Avoidance recommendation - MANEUVER, MONITOR, or NOMINAL based on miss distance.
SGP4
Simplified General Perturbations - standard model for satellite orbit propagation from TLE data.
Inclination
Angle between orbital plane and Earths equator. SSO orbits at ~98 deg, equatorial at ~0 deg.
Apogee/Perigee
Highest and lowest points in an elliptical orbit, measured in km from Earths center.
